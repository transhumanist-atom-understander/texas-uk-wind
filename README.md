The United Kingdom generates less wind power than Texas every year, despite having over twice the population:

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/lesswrong-2-0/image/upload/v1794984516/lexical_client_uploads/jwnff718exvitkng1ipc.png)

(Texas data from [ERCOT](https://www.ercot.com/gridinfo/generation), UK data from [OWID](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/electricity-mix?tab=line&time=2007..latest&country=%7EGBR&mapSelect=%7EGBR&source=wind&metric=generation&frequency=annual), code in [the repo for this post](https://github.com/transhumanist-atom-understander/texas-uk-wind).)

I'm just irritated by people citing the UK as a major renewable energy success, always quoting relative numbers.
Like: in 2025, 29% of UK electricity came from wind, how impressive.
Well, in Texas it was 24%, but that's more impressive than 29% of UK electricity because the UK doesn't generate that much electricity.

While the UK doesn't need as much electricity as Texas, because they don't have to air condition houses in the Texas heat, they need more than they're generating.
They could use some air conditioning, first of all.
They don't have dryers.
Or, have them but don't run them, [like this guy on r/AskUK](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskUK/comments/10o6hll/comment/j6e1x5m/), because of the electricity bill.

There was [a BBC article](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c86ey5n9vx9o) where they talked to a guy who had a heat pump installed, but went back to using the gas boiler, because it was so expensive to power the heat pump.
This kind of thing is why Brits can't spin their low electricity use as enlightened and environmentally friendly.
They have big plans for heat pumps, electric cars, hydrogen, but can't power them.

So I'm irritated by those trumpeting the UK as a renewable energy success, but I'm also irritated by the opposition to wind.
As if the issue is that in an insane spending spree, too many wind turbines were built, when really it is just not that much.
These people blend in with the opposition to building anything at all.

Texas's wind capacity has been much cheaper to build because it is entirely onshore, whereas UK wind generation was 60% offshore in 2025.
We must forgive the UK for having less onshore wind than Texas, because they have less land.
But Texas has also pulled ahead in _density_ of onshore wind generation, with about 20% more generation per unit area than the UK in 2025:

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/lesswrong-2-0/image/upload/v1794984532/lexical_client_uploads/aidbpbgdxmaouca5yw5b.png)

What went wrong?
Building onshore wind in England was de facto illegal for years, and is still proceeding slowly.
Onshore wind farms were still being built in Scotland and Wales, which created the problem of transporting the power.
Sometimes I think it's more important that the Texans are allowed to build power lines than that they are allowed to build generation.
One scandal of the UK grid is how much wind power is generated but not delivered.
But more importantly, I wonder how much we blame on NIMBY opposition to wind developments, which we should blame on slow construction of power lines.
I suppose that when it's hard to build power lines, wind developments are constrained to be near population centers, exposing themselves to more opposition.

In a wider sense, I don't know what went wrong.
Something about culture, financing, government, I don't know.

All this is just what I want you to have in mind when you hear about the _fraction_ of UK power generated from wind.
That should sound like "over half as tall as Danny Devito" or whatever other underwhelming comparison.
